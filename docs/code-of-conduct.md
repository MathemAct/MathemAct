# Code of conduct

This is the full text, adapted from the [Contributor Covenant][covenant] version 2.1. The
page at [/code-of-conduct/](../src/pages/code-of-conduct.astro) is the reader-facing version:
shorter, in this site's voice, and written to be read before somebody decides whether to take
part. **The two must say the same thing.** Where they differ, this document governs, and the
page is what needs fixing.

Four sections here have no counterpart in the Covenant, and they are the reason this is an
adaptation rather than a copy. Pseudonymity, third-party material, weaponising a disclosure,
and the difference between scepticism and contempt are the specific hazards of a site that
asks professional mathematicians to describe, in public, how much they rely on AI.

[covenant]: https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct.html

---

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors and moderators pledge to make participation in MathemAct a
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste,
colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse,
inclusive and healthy community.

We pledge specifically that **using an AI tool in mathematical work, to any degree, is not a
thing anyone here will be judged for.** The corpus exists because that judgement is real
elsewhere and is keeping people from saying what they actually do.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Arguing with the account rather than with the person. *"This verification step does not
  rule out the error I would worry about"* is what this site is for. *"Anyone who trusts a
  chatbot with a proof is not a serious mathematician"* is not: the first can be answered,
  the second only makes people stop posting.
- Assuming an account is honest until there is reason not to. People here are describing
  their own work, often work that did not go well.
- Reporting failures. An account of something that did not work is a first-class
  contribution and the corpus is short of them.
- Giving and accepting correction gracefully. Being wrong in public about what a tool did is
  ordinary, and the correction belongs in a comment.
- Focusing on what is best for the community as a whole rather than for one's own position
  in a debate about AI.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their
  explicit permission.
- **Attempting to identify a pseudonymous contributor**, speculating publicly about who
  someone is, or hinting that you have guessed. See below.
- **Using someone's disclosure against them** — in a referee report, a hiring discussion, a
  grant panel, or online.
- **Reproducing third-party unpublished material**: a referee report, somebody else's
  preprint, private correspondence, in a transcript or a quotation.
- **Fabrication**: posting an account of work you did not do, or inventing a transcript, an
  outcome, or a verification step.
- Promotion of a product, a service, or yourself in place of a genuine account.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  setting.

### Pseudonymity is absolute

Admitting to heavy AI reliance still carries professional risk, and some of the most valuable
accounts here will only ever be posted under a pseudonym. A pseudonymous contributor may hold
an institutional badge: the badge attests that an address at that institution was confirmed,
and says nothing about who holds it.

Do not try to work out who someone is. Do not speculate publicly. Do not hint that you have
guessed. This is the rule most likely to get an account removed with no warning first.

### Disagreement is not misconduct

Reporting that a widely praised tool failed you, or that a widely condemned one worked, is a
contribution. Scepticism about AI in mathematics is the founding position of this project and
will never be treated as a breach. Contempt for the people using it is a different thing, and
is.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Moderators are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards and will take
appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive or harmful.

Moderators have the right and responsibility to hide comments, reports, claims and
other contributions that are not aligned with this code of conduct, and will communicate
reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

**These standards apply to moderators identically.** A moderator's own contributions go
through the same review as anybody else's — the database refuses a self-approval by name —
and a complaint about a moderator is handled by a different one.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies within all community spaces: everything posted on MathemAct —
reports, comments, claims, ratings, flags — and messages sent to the addresses on
this site. It also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in
public spaces.

It does not extend to a person's conduct elsewhere in mathematics, with one exception: using
a disclosure made here against its author anywhere is in scope, because the disclosure was
made here and the harm depends on that.

## Enforcement

Flag abusive, harassing or otherwise unacceptable behaviour to the moderators at
**<matesimpatica@gmail.com>**. Every report and comment can also be flagged from the page
it appears on, which puts it in the same queue.

Use the address rather than the in-page control when the flag concerns a moderator, or when
you would rather it did not go through the site.

Tell us what you saw and where. You do not have to explain why it upset you and you will not
be asked to justify flagging something.

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. Moderation here is
volunteer-run, so it is careful rather than fast: a flag may take days. Content that could
do real harm while it is looked at is hidden first, and hiding something is not a finding
against anyone.

All moderators are obliged to respect the privacy and security of whoever flagged
an incident. The flag queue is readable only by moderators, and a flag is never shown next
to the content it names — a visible flag count would turn flagging into a downvote.

## Enforcement guidelines

Moderators will follow these guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they
deem in violation of this code of conduct.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact.** Use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed unprofessional
or unwelcome.

**Consequence.** A private, written warning from a moderator, providing clarity around the
nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public
apology may be requested. Where content is involved it may be hidden, with the reason
recorded.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact.** A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the
people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the code of conduct,
for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interaction in community spaces as
well as external channels. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Community impact.** A serious violation of community standards, including sustained
inappropriate behaviour.

**Consequence.** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with
the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the
people involved is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent
ban. On this site a ban blocks posting, commenting, rating, confirming and flagging; it does
not remove what was already contributed.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact.** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or
disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence.** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

**Attempting to deanonymise a pseudonymous contributor, and harassment, may result in a
permanent ban at first offence.** These do not get a warning first, and the reason is that
the harm is done the moment it happens and cannot be undone by a later apology.

## Appeals

Write to <matesimpatica@gmail.com>. A decision will be reconsidered by a moderator other than
the one who made it, wherever there is another to ask. You will be told what rule was applied
and why; the internal moderation log is not published, and the sentence written to you is the
explanation you are owed. See [moderation.md](moderation.md).
